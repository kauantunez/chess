How the knight and the pawn move

Your win: you know every square a knight can jump to, and you can tell where a pawn can move and capture.

Written from general knowledge, not a verified source (FIDE and Lichess are still unreachable). Re-check against FIDE Laws of Chess, Article 3. Pawn promotion and en passant are left for a later lesson.

The rules

  • Knight ♘: moves in an "L": two squares in one direction, then one square sideways. It is the only piece that jumps over others. It lands on the square, and captures what is there.
  • Pawn ♙: moves straight forward one square, never backward. From its starting row it may move two squares. It cannot move forward onto an occupied square.
  • Pawn captures differently: one square diagonally forward, and only to take an enemy piece.

Practice: tap every square

The white pawn moves up the board. A black pawn ♟ is an enemy.
