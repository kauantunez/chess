Set up the board correctly

Your win: you can set up a chess board from scratch, and spot a wrong setup at a glance.

Heads up: written without a verified source (the FIDE rules site was unreachable). Facts here are well-known basics, but I'll re-check them against the FIDE Laws of Chess when it is reachable.

1. Turn the board the right way

The board has 64 squares, alternating pale and dark. Sit so that a pale square is in your bottom-right corner. A quick way to remember: "pale on the right".

2. Place the pieces

Each player has 16 pieces. Put the pawns on the whole second row. On the back row, from the corners inward: rook, knight, bishop, then the queen and king in the middle. The queen goes on her own colour (white queen on a pale square, black queen on a dark square). The two sides are mirror images.

White (hollow pieces) at the bottom, black at the top. White moves first.

Practice (do it for real)

Set up a real board or a free online board from memory, then compare with the diagram. Check the three common mistakes: board turned wrong, king and queen swapped, pawns on the wrong row.
